Regular Grammars

Definition

A regular grammar is a context-free grammar G = (V, Σ, P, S) where all the rules have one of the following forms:
  1. A → a (non-terminal → terminal)
  2. A → aB (non-terminal → terminal non-terminal)
  3. A → ε (non-terminal → ε)

With 'A' and 'B' being members of V and 'a' being a member of Σ, a fourth possible form appears:

  1. A → B (non-terminal → non-terminal)

Example

A regular grammar that generates the language (ab)⁺a* | ε.

S → aB | ε
B → bS | bA
A → aA | ε
